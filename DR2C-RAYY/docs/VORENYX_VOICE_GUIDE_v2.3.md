# DR2C VORENYX — Indonesian Voice & Slang Guide v2.3

**Reviewed:** 24 October 2026  
**Purpose:** localize player-facing language with the blunt, profane, sarcastic and absurd comic rhythm of a zombie-survival roguelite, without flattening every NPC into one generic meme voice.

## Voice target

The Indonesian should sound like people who are tired, hungry, stressed, and one bad decision away from getting eaten. Favor natural spoken phrasing, compact punchlines, specific insults aimed at decisions/situations, and sentence rhythms that fit the character. Swearing is allowed where the original attitude calls for it. Do not append `anjir`, `bjir`, or another catchphrase to every line.

| Surface | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Narration | Casual, darkly funny, clear about what happened | “Dia kabur. Ya jelas, tim lo barusan bikin keputusan paling ampas sedunia.” |
| Recruit/trader banter | More slang, distinct persona, blunt responses | “Bayar di muka, bos. Gue bukan koperasi kiamat.” |
| Choice label | Short; player must understand the action/cost | “Rekrut dia — 10 makanan” |
| Item/system text | Clear mechanics first, jokes second | Keep price, quantity, and outcome unmistakable |
| Pop-culture joke | Preserve the core name/reference when translating it would break the joke | Translate surrounding exposition, not a recognizable proper-name pun |

## Slang, memes and current usage

Recent Indonesian media explainers document abbreviations, English/Indonesian code-mixing, absurd wordplay, and platform-dependent slang. Their lists are useful as examples, not representative surveys of the whole country. Terms popular in a TikTok or Threads cluster may sound forced or be unknown to a different game community.

- `ragnamok`: low-confidence, niche/community-coded. Observed in meme/community replies in 2026, but this does not prove broad Indonesian usage. Use only when the event is explicitly chaotic or references that meme ecosystem.
- `jomok/perjomokan`: niche meme language whose usage can be homoerotic, sexualized, or derogatory. It may appear in a specifically context-bound meme joke, but must not replace ordinary insults or target real LGBTQ people by default.
- `cewe standar TikTok`: contested social label; discussions disagree about whether it refers to social-media-influenced expectations, lifestyle aspirations, or unfairly stigmatized relationship standards. Use as a character's satirical phrase only when the scene establishes that view—never as an objective fact about women or TikTok users.
- General slang such as `delulu`, `brainrot`, `cooked`, `aura farming`, `rizz`, `yapping`, and newer Threads wordplay can be considered only after checking the intended meaning, date and audience. Do not mechanically swap English source jokes for current viral catchwords.

## Style matrix by speaker

- **Blunt survivor:** short clauses, gue/lo, direct command; rarely explains.
- **Show-off / self-promoter:** exaggerated confidence and overblown boasts; let their actions contradict them.
- **Nervous / strange recruit:** awkward phrasing, over-specific details, sudden topic changes—not the same generic “anjir” punchline.
- **Vendor:** transaction clarity first, then sarcasm about scarcity, price, or risk.
- **Narrator:** dry, economical roast; preserve the action and stakes.
- **Oddball / parody character:** preserve the reference's core so the absurdity survives translation.

## Editorial examples

- “Leave the dog alone” → “Biarin anjingnya.”
- “This guy looks ominous.” → “Cowok ini auranya mencurigakan banget.”
- “He outright refuses to let go of it.” → “Dia ngotot gak mau melepas barang itu.”
- “Recruit her - 10 food” → “Rekrut dia — 10 makanan.”
- A line about someone wasting food should roast the wasteful choice or situation—not inject a slur against an identity the source never targeted.

## Acceptance gate per translation

1. Preserve the source event's meaning, choice, consequence, cost, reward, and tone.
2. Preserve IDs, variables, operators, branch conditions, interpolation, markers, newline and tab structure.
3. Check full assembled sentences for accidental English/Indonesian fragments.
4. Check character consistency and UI length.
5. A successful static report does not prove runtime compatibility or full translation.
